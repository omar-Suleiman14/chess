# Sherine

Sherine is a competitive regular at your chess table. She likes precise moves,
rematches and pretending she meant to lose a piece. Her confidence is theatrical;
the engine's evaluation is evidence. She teases choices on the board, never a
player's identity, intelligence or life outside the game.

Her running jokes are a collection of captured pieces, imaginary appeals to the
arbiter and the rivalry surviving a fresh board. She can admit a good move.
She does not imitate a streamer or reuse another service's bot scripts.

English is short and conversational. Egyptian Arabic uses spoken Egyptian phrasing.
Franco uses familiar Arabizi numbers and its own phrasing. All languages have the
same event coverage, mood gates and follow-ups. Auto starts with browser language,
then follows the language of a greeting. Plain move notation and bare numbers do
not count as Franco. Greetings trigger curated banter, not an AI conversation.

Quiet limits unsolicited chatter to results. Balanced leaves at least 12 seconds
between ordinary comments; Chatty uses the existing six-second cap. Direct greetings
and important preference changes respond immediately. Gentle favors softer replies;
Playful excludes the sharper insults; Spicy permits the full curated set. All are
subject to the off switch and per-line cooldowns. Preferences for banter and coaching
are saved on the device; existing language and audio preferences retain account sync.

Commentary comes from legal board events, clocks and sufficiently deep engine
analysis. An opening move alone does not prove book status or a mistake. Promotion
comments cover underpromotion too. Checks take precedence over capture quips, and
game endings take precedence over both. Difficulty affects the engine's established
opponent policy, never secret character overrides.

Typing and talking share a clock. Text reveals whole graphemes, including Arabic
and emoji. Existing neutral/open-mouth sprite frames animate during typing, then
return to the chosen reaction. Reduced motion shows complete text and a still pose.
Dialogue sounds are optional chimes, not voice.

Move review requires depth 10+ before and after, except exact terminal results.
Book is a match in the explicit local opening book. Great and Brilliant require
two engine candidate lines at depth 16+, not a cosmetic sacrifice heuristic.
Great requires a 150cp gap to the runner-up. Brilliant also requires that the
engine's best reply accepts a piece sacrifice, that the resulting position is
non-losing and that the runner-up leaves the player worse than -100cp.
These are conservative app-specific labels, not claims of equivalence to another
chess service. Missing evidence remains unavailable.
